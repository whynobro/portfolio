Michael Fischbach

Mechanical engineering · Cal Poly San Luis Obispo

In 2019 I ordered a concrete fingerboard ramp from Germany for $30. My mom looked at it and said I could make it myself, so I did, with old molding material from the garage. That garage ramp turned into Chameleon Ramps, which I ran for six years and which shipped 627 orders to 15 countries. Every product went through four to six prototype generations before the mold was good enough to sell from, and I still work that way.

Now I lead Cal Poly's 12-person WERC team designing a 1 kW wireless charger for NASA's Artemis lunar rover, and I intern at All Domain Integration supporting Space Force and Air Force mission systems engineers. The work I like best is still the garage kind: a drawing on a screen turning into a part I can hold.

About

Profile

I am a mechanical engineering student at Cal Poly San Luis Obispo, in the Honors Program, with a 3.79 GPA and a place on the President's Honors List for my first year.

The work I like best is where a drawing becomes a part: design it, make it, test it, find out where the model was wrong, change it. I learn the most when something fails the first time. On the wave energy converter the first gear ratio was wrong, the motor could not turn against it, and only the second one ran. That applies to a part on a mill and to a system in production, which is why both appear here.

I grew up in an Air Force family and moved nine times across seven states and territories, so a new team or a new city is normal. Away from engineering: Eagle Scout, 500 miles of backpacking, rowing with the Channel Islands club, and watercolour.

# Pocket Gallery (the handheld)

The toy that replaced tic-tac-toe (2026-10-25). A DS-style, Pokemon-like game:
walk one gallery hall, meet a curator ("trainer") for each work on the wall,
battle by asking about its figures, earn coins, spend them at the gift shop.

## Rules

- **Original art only.** No Nintendo sprites, names, music, ROMs or
  screenshots in the repo, not even as references. "Fainted", HP bars and the
  two-screen shell are genre conventions; Pokemon names and creatures are not.
- **Every figure is the site's.** `data.ts` quotes `src/i18n/en.ts` (the
  `case.*` keys). `scripts/verify-handheld.mjs` fails CI on a number that is
  not in `en.ts` (or in its small `FACTS` list, each with a source). WERC
  POWER moves must say "model". Bot test count: never quoted here.
- **Six works = six trainers**, in `PROJECTS` order. Off-wall works (water,
  Doorknob-Inator) are not in the game.

## How a battle works

`fight()` in `game.ts`: wipe in, trainer and player slide on, the trainer
throws a gilt frame and the work appears (scale-in from white), plates slide
in. Each of the 4 moves quotes one figure and removes 1/4 HP (blink, shake,
drain; the HP bar goes green, yellow, red). The animation is chosen by the
move's type (`attack()`): POWER bolt, MACHINING sparks, BUSINESS coins,
SOFTWARE 0/1 rain, CAD wireframe squares, DESIGN confetti, TEAM stars,
MARKETS candles, CONTROLS/ENERGY pulses. When every move is asked the work
faints, the trainer returns, coins pay out (100 first win, 25 rematch, 10 for
reading a work's label) and the visitor levels up. RUN leaves at any time.
VIEW ROOM closes the dialog and opens that room on the site.

Battles start by walking into a trainer's line of sight (3 tiles, first
meeting only), by talking to one, or by tapping a work on the bottom screen
(so a recruiter never has to walk).

## Art

A `Sprite` is a character grid plus palette; `$slot` colours (skin, top...)
resolve per character, which is how one body becomes every trainer and how
outfits recolour the player. Stack orders the art assumes:
overworld body, outfit, extras, hair, hat; battle back body, outfit, tool, hat.

The art files were GENERATED by scripts now archived (gitignored) in
`assets-src/handheld/generators/`. Two outputs (`tiles.ts`, `font.ts`) were
then hand-fixed for `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, so the committed `.ts` is the
source of truth: edit it directly, or re-run a generator (paths inside are
hard-coded to the old worktree) and re-apply the strictness fixes.

Preview any art module as a contact sheet (also validates the grids):

```sh
node scripts/handheld-preview.mjs src/handheld/art/monsters.ts out.png --scale=4
```

The battle backdrop takes ~0.8 s to paint the first time, so `game.ts`
paints it once into a canvas, 600 ms after the game opens.

## Input

Arrows/WASD walk (and move the bottom-screen focus in battle and the shop),
Z/Enter/Space = A, X/Backspace = B, Esc closes the dialog. Tapping the top
screen = A; the bottom screen is tapped directly. A hidden button list
mirrors every bottom-screen region for screen readers. Reduced motion skips
tweens, typing and particles; walking still works.

`window.__pocket.state()` / `.activate(key)` exist for the playtest script.
